# OKR set

Most OKR templates give you empty boxes and a scoring formula. This one gives
you the four questions that make the difference between an OKR set that changes
behaviour and one that gets copy-pasted next quarter.

One objective. Two or three key results. If you have more than three, you have
priorities you haven't chosen between yet.

## The template

```
OBJECTIVE — the change in the world, in plain language
  <One sentence a new joiner could repeat. No numbers, no jargon, no solutions.>

  Why now: <what makes this the thing, this cycle?>
  Who's better off: <specific people, not "the business">

KEY RESULT 1 — the primary measure
  <measure>: from <baseline> to <target> by <date>
  First signal expected: <a smaller, earlier reading — week 3, not week 12>

KEY RESULT 2
  <measure>: from <baseline> to <target> by <date>

KEY RESULT 3 (optional — resist)
  <measure>: from <baseline> to <target> by <date>

GUARDRAILS — must not get worse
  <measure> stays <threshold>
  <measure> stays <threshold>

THE FOUR LENSES
  Better value — what value, for whom, and how would they describe it?
  Sooner ------- what could we learn in the first three weeks?
  Safer -------- is it safe to attempt, safe to challenge, safe to miss?
  Happier ------ would this team choose this objective if given the choice?

RHYTHM
  Owner: <one name — accountable for the learning, not for hitting the number>
  Review: <cadence and format — e.g. 20 min fortnightly, evidence not status>
  Stop rule: <what we'd see that would make us drop or change this>
```

## Worked example — a retailer's fulfilment group

> **Objective:** Customers stop being surprised by where their order actually is.
>
> *Why now:* "Where is my order" is 38% of contact volume and the top driver of
> one-star reviews this year.
> *Who's better off:* customers waiting on a delivery, and the care team who
> currently apologise for a system they can't see into.
>
> **KR1 (primary):** "Where is my order" contacts per 1,000 orders, from 94 to
> 60 by end of cycle. *First signal:* week 3 reading on the 15% of orders in the
> pilot region.
> **KR2:** Share of orders where the promised date matches the delivered date,
> from 71% to 85%.
>
> **Guardrails:** delivery cost per order flat; care team CSAT doesn't drop
> (we're removing their contacts, not their ability to help).
>
> **Four lenses:**
> *Better value* — customers can plan their day. *Sooner* — the pilot region
> gives a reading in three weeks. *Safer* — no headcount consequence attached to
> the number; two team members already argued the target is too aggressive and
> we wrote their reasoning next to the KR. *Happier* — care agents asked for
> this in the last retro.
>
> **Rhythm:** owner is the fulfilment product lead. Fortnightly 20-minute review
> looking at the trend line and one customer recording — not a RAG status.
> **Stop rule:** if week-6 pilot data shows contacts flat, the cause isn't
> visibility and we take the promise-accuracy problem head-on instead.

## Worked example — an engineering organisation

> **Objective:** Teams can ship a small change on the day they decide to make it.
>
> *Why now:* our last three incidents were all large, batched releases.
> *Who's better off:* engineers who currently wait for a release train, and
> customers who wait weeks for small fixes.
>
> **KR1 (primary):** median lead time from merge to production, from 6 days to
> under 1 day across the 9 teams in the group. *First signal:* two volunteer
> teams reporting by week 2.
> **KR2:** share of deploys made outside the release-train window, from 5% to
> 50%.
>
> **Guardrails:** change-failure rate stays at or below today's 12%; on-call
> pages per week don't increase.
>
> **Four lenses:**
> *Better value* — faster correction of customer-visible problems. *Sooner* —
> volunteer teams give a signal in a fortnight. *Safer* — change-failure rate is
> an explicit guardrail so "ship faster" can't quietly mean "ship worse"; the
> number is never used in performance reviews. *Happier* — this came from the
> engineering survey's top-scoring frustration.
>
> **Rhythm:** owner is the delivery infrastructure lead. Monthly review with two
> teams demoing their actual pipeline. **Stop rule:** if the volunteer teams
> can't move, the constraint is architectural, not procedural — and we'd re-cut
> the goal around a single service boundary.

## Pressure test before you commit

Read the set back and ask:

1. Could we hit every key result and have nothing be better for anyone?
2. Is there exactly **one** primary measure, or three co-equal ones?
3. Is anything here a project plan with a percentage attached?
4. What's the earliest date we'd know we're wrong? If it's the deadline, re-cut.
5. Who in the room thinks this is the wrong objective — and did that change it?

If question 5 has no answer, the OKR set isn't ready. Safety to challenge is the
best predictor of goal quality there is.
